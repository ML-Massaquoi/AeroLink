what do you know about cable and port management software for airport
Yes. Cable and Port Management Software (CPMS) is an important type of infrastructure management system, especially in airports where IT, security, telecommunications, operational systems, and facilities depend on thousands of physical connections.

At an airport like Freetown International Airport (FIA), this type of software could be extremely useful for managing network infrastructure, identifying where cables terminate, monitoring ports, documenting connections, and helping the IT team troubleshoot faults without manually tracing everything.

1. What is Cable and Port Management Software?
It is a centralized system used to manage, document, track, and monitor an organization's physical network infrastructure.

It helps IT staff answer questions such as:

Where does this network cable originate?

Which patch panel does it terminate on?

Which switch is connected to this patch panel?

Which switch port connects to a particular office, camera, AP, or system?

Is this port occupied, available, disabled, or faulty?

What device is connected to Switch 03, Port 24?

If I disconnect this cable, what systems could go offline?

Where are the fiber connections between buildings?

Who last changed this connection?

Instead of relying on technicians' memory, handwritten labels, Excel spreadsheets, or outdated diagrams, the airport maintains a digital record of its physical network infrastructure.

7. Does it automatically detect connected devices?
This is an important distinction.

There are two levels of cable and port management.


Level 1 — Documentation-based

No specialized hardware required
IT staff physically verify connections and update the system. The system maintains the digital infrastructure map and connection records.

パッチ接続自動認識システム『VM7 Auto-Patch Manager』 | ケーブル&ファシリティマネジメント | エフ・アイ・ティー・パシフィック株式会社
Level 2 — Intelligent monitoring

Additional hardware / integration
Smart patch panels or supported network integrations can automatically detect certain connection changes and update connectivity information.

Level 1: Software-based management
The IT team physically surveys the airport, identifies equipment and cables, labels everything, and enters the infrastructure into the system.

The software then provides:

Accurate documentation

Port availability

Cable tracking

Search

Connection tracing

Maintenance history

Change management

Reports

This is the most practical way to start without purchasing expensive intelligent patching hardware.

Level 2: Automated monitoring and discovery
The system can integrate with managed network switches through technologies such as:

SNMP

Switch APIs

LLDP

MAC address tables

Network monitoring systems

Depending on device support and permissions, this can help automatically discover devices, identify active switch ports, collect port statistics, and detect certain network changes.

However, detecting an active switch port does not necessarily prove the complete physical cable path. To accurately track patch-panel cross-connects, those connections must be documented or supported by intelligent patching hardware.
